## 1. One idea, two ways to play

Both modes use the same world (Aurelis), creatures, battles, homestead and boats. They differ in who decides what happens next.

| | **STORY MODE** | **YOUR OWN ADVENTURE** |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Save the vale: relight the Great Lantern, face the Wildcard | Whatever you pick: a Path (see 4), or none |
| Pace | A guided chain of chapters, 8 Arenas as the spine | You choose where to go and what to do first |
| Gating | Badges and story flags open the map | Badges still open the map, but you can also unlock areas other ways (3) |
| NPCs | Fixed cast with scripted scenes | Same cast, but their requests change with your Path |
| Ending | One ending with small variations | Several endings, depending on your Path and choices |
| Save | Own slot | Own slot (you can run both at once) |

**Start screen:** NEW GAME asks STORY MODE or YOUR OWN ADVENTURE. Your world map, homestead and party carry over only inside one slot. **[DECIDE]** Should a finished Story Mode slot let you "continue in Your Own Adventure" with everything you earned?

## 2. STORY MODE: the script

### Premise (keeps the existing setup, adds a real plot)

You are a Warden-in-training in **Lanternrest**. The **Great Lantern** has kept the vale's creatures calm for 300 years. It is dying and the legendary **Wildcard**, **Joker**, has vanished.

New twist so it isn't the usual "beat 8 gyms": the Lantern is fed by **three shards**, and the shards are not just lying around. Each one is held by something that *chose* to hold it. Collecting them is about earning trust, not taking.

**The antagonist** is **Warden Vesper**, your old mentor's former apprentice. Vesper believes creatures should be kept calm by force, not by trust, and wants the Lantern to burn forever with the Wildcard bound to it. Vesper isn't evil in a cartoon way: they are *right* that the vale is in danger, and wrong about the cure. Their crew, the **Ashen Wardens**, appear in every region.

**Rival: Tam**, who starts at the same hearth, picks the type that is strong against yours, and slowly learns the same lesson you do. **[DECIDE]** Rival gender/name/look. I have used a neutral "Tam" as a placeholder.

### Chapter outline

Each chapter: where you go, what happens, what it unlocks.

**Prologue: The Dimming (Lanternrest)**
- Wake up. Lanterns flicker. Elder Wren leads you to the Hearth Shrine and your **Bondling** hatches (your starter follows you).
- Tam arrives, picks theirs, and challenges you to a first battle on the green.
- Wren gives you the **Warden's Lamp** (a story item: lights dark caves, glows near shards) and tells you to ask the Wardens in the nearby towns for help.
- Unlocks: Sunmeadow and the road to Stonebridge. Your cabin plot is introduced.

**Chapter 1: First Light (Stonebridge, Rock Arena, Leader Mason)**
- Mason wants proof you can keep a creature safe, not just win. Arena puzzle plus battle.
- First Ashen Warden grunts appear, "borrowing" the town's creatures for Vesper.
- **Reward:** Badge 1, the Climb item, first village-upgrade: your homestead gets a nursery.
- Unlocks: Evergreen Forest and Whispering Woods.

**Chapter 2: The Green Hush (Sunhaven, Grass; Evergreen Forest)**
- Ferna's forest has gone silent: the creatures are frightened. You trace it to a dimmed lantern-tree.
- **Shard 1** is held by a forest guardian creature. You don't fight it first: you calm it (a small gentle minigame: approach, offer a berry, don't run).
- Vesper appears in person for the first time, polite and unsettling.
- **Reward:** Badge 2, Shard 1, Rowboat recipe. Unlocks Driftwood Port, Sandsreach.

**Chapter 3: Tides and Winds (Driftwood Port, Flying; first sea voyage)**
- Zephyra's lighthouse is out. Ferry to Verdant Isle. You learn boats and the sea map.
- Tam fights beside you against Ashen boats. Their first moment of doubt about Vesper.
- **Reward:** Badge 3, motorboat, access to Cloudpeak, Tidefall, Verdant Isle.

**Chapter 4: The Quiet Water (Lakeside, Water; Tidefall Islands)**
- Marlo's lake has been drained by a stolen dam stone. Islands in Tidefall are cut off.
- **Shard 2** sits in a drowned shrine reached by boat and a dive. Ashen Wardens race you to it.
- **Reward:** Badge 4, Shard 2, sailboat. Cobalt City and the Badlands open.

**Chapter 5: Dust and Thunder (Sandsreach, Ground; Cobalt City, Electric)**
- Osha and Rayne each ask you to choose: help *their* town first. Either order works. **[DECIDE]** Do you want branching order here even in Story Mode?
- Vesper's plan becomes clear: the Wardens are stealing creature "calm" from every town to power a machine in the Badlands.
- **Reward:** Badges 5 and 6, flight (a creature that can carry you).

**Chapter 6: Cold Resolve (Frostmere, Ice; Glacier Pass; Aurora Isles)**
- Isolde guards the pass. **Shard 3** is on the Aurora Isles, guarded by a creature that only appears if Tam is by your side.
- Tam turns on Vesper here.
- **Reward:** Badge 7, Shard 3.

**Chapter 7: Fire at the Heart (Emberfall Volcano, Fire; Pyrra)**
- Vesper's machine is under the volcano. Pyrra helps you break in. You win the final badge.
- Vesper escapes to Cloudpeak. **Reward:** Badge 8.

**Chapter 8: The Great Lantern (Cloudpeak castle, then Lanternrest)**
- Climb the castle. Face Vesper (a 3-stage battle, each stage with a different creature of theirs and a line of dialogue).
- Return to Lanternrest, place the three shards. The Lantern relights. The **Hollow Gate** opens in the southeast.

**Final: The Wildcard (Hollow Gate)**
- **Joker** is not a villain: it is the vale itself, tired, testing you.
- The last fight has no "defeat" requirement: you win by calming it with your bonded creature and everyone you've helped (a final choice of 3 stances: **Trust**, **Strength**, **Balance**).
- Epilogue shows what happened to each town based on your choices. Credits roll; the game keeps going (post-game below).

### Post-game (Story Mode)
- Rematches with all 8 Leaders (stronger teams, new items).
- The **Wildcard** can be befriended.
- The Warden's Lamp becomes a map tool: reveals hidden shards, secret caves, rare creatures.
- A new homestead tier (Warden's Hall) and cosmetic rewards.

### How the player moves through it (the "loop" in Story Mode)
1. **Quest marker:** the map screen has a "Story" pin that always shows your next goal, and a short journal line ("Ask Ferna about the silent forest"). It can be switched off. **[DECIDE]** Always on, or off by default?
2. Travel by foot, then boat, then flight as chapters give you each.
3. In each region: talk to the Warden, deal with the local Ashen trouble, beat the Arena, get the badge and shard hint, return to the homestead (optional) and move on.
4. The map tells you where you're blocked: padlocks on locked exits show the badge or story event needed.

## 3. YOUR OWN ADVENTURE: how it works

No fixed plot. You pick a **Starting Path** at the beginning, and it can change later. The world is the same, but *what the world asks of you* is generated from your Path.

### Starting Paths (pick one, or "Drifter" for none)

| Path | You are | Core goals | Unique flavour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Champion** | A challenger | Beat all 8 Leaders, then a rival tournament | Arena rematches, ranked ladder, trainers recognise you |
| **Explorer** | A surveyor | Fill the map, find every creature, discover secrets | Map reveals as you travel, bonus for first visits, hidden caves |
| **Homesteader** | A builder/farmer | Grow your base into a village | Quests from the shops and farms; visitors move in; trade routes |
| **Sailor** | A seafarer | Chart the Aurelian Sea | Harbours, islands, trade, treasure maps, boat upgrades |
| **Collector / Researcher** | A naturalist | Complete the Dex; study behaviour | Field notes, breeding/nursery goals, rare event spawns |
| **Warden** | A protector | Same story as Story Mode, but optional and non-linear | Gets the three shards in any order |
| **Drifter** | Free | Nothing assigned | Random "Wanderer's Requests" appear in every town |

You can **switch Path any time** from a lodge or your homestead (a small cost in time or money, not progress): a Champion who gets bored can become a Homesteader and keep everything.

### Unlocking things without the story

In Story Mode, the next chapter unlocks the next area. In Your Own Adventure you can also unlock areas through **Reputation**:

- **Region Reputation:** each region tracks how well you're known (helping people, catching creatures, trading, farming). High enough reputation opens that region's gates without a badge ("Locals vouch for you").
- **Badges still work** as an alternate key.
- **Passes and charters** can be bought or earned (a ferry charter, a mountain permit).
- A single hard rule so it never feels broken: the **Hollow Gate** only opens when the Lantern is relit, in every mode.

### The "Create Your Own Adventure" engine

Instead of a script, the game rolls **adventure threads** from a library. A thread is a short chain (3 to 6 steps) with a start trigger, choices and a payoff.

Examples:
- **The Missing Ferry:** a captain's boat is gone. Search three harbours, choose between returning it or selling it. Reputation changes either way.
- **The Sick Orchard:** your farm's trees wither. Trace the cause (a creature migrated, a rival's runoff), then choose to relocate it or fix the source.
- **Trader's Debt:** carry goods to a far island on a deadline; a storm forces a detour that uncovers a secret.
- **Duel at Dawn:** a rival challenges you to a battle chain across three towns.

Rules:
- Threads appear at an NPC (a "!" on the map, or a note on the lodge board).
- At most 3 active at once; a journal tracks them.
- Choices have lasting effects (an NPC moves to your homestead, a shop changes stock, a route opens).
- Threads are tagged by Path, so a Sailor sees more sea threads, a Homesteader more building ones, but anyone can accept any thread.

### A player's day in Your Own Adventure
1. Wake at the homestead or a lodge. The **Lodge Board** shows 3 to 5 offers, new ones daily.
2. Pick what interests you (or ignore them all and just explore).
3. Travel: foot, boat, flight, vehicles. Creatures roam the world as normal.
4. Return, sell, upgrade, rest, take new offers.

Endings: when you hit a Path's **Finale** (e.g. Champion: win the Aurelis Cup; Homesteader: your base becomes a town of 12 residents; Sailor: chart every harbour and find the lost island; Warden: relight the Lantern), the game shows a Finale scene and a tailored epilogue. After that you keep playing.

## 4. What I'd need to build (and what already exists)

**Already in the game and reused:** world map, towns, 8 Arenas and Leaders, badges and gates, boats and sea map, flight, vehicles, homestead and structures, shards and Lantern, creatures and battles, save system, Test Mode.

**New systems (rough size):**

| System | What it is | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Mode select + two save slots | Start screen, slot data | Small |
| Quest/journal engine | Quests with steps, flags, rewards; journal screen; map pin | Medium |
| Story cutscenes | Dialogue scenes, rival and Vesper battles | Medium-Large (the most writing) |
| Thread library (adventure mode) | Template engine + 20 to 30 starter threads | Large |
| Reputation system | Per-region reputation, gate unlocks | Medium |
| Paths | Path pick/switch, finales, epilogues | Medium |
| Lodge Board | Daily offers UI | Small-Medium |
| Post-game | Rematches, Lamp tool, Warden's Hall | Medium |

**Suggested build order (so you can play each stage):**
1. Quest engine + journal + map pin + mode select.
2. Story Mode prologue to Chapter 2 (playable slice, so you can judge tone).
3. The rest of Story Mode, chapter by chapter.
4. Reputation, Paths and the Lodge Board.
5. First 10 adventure threads, then grow to 30.
6. Finales, epilogues, post-game.
